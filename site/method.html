taste algorithm: tullie murrell's hn for-you feed, cosine to the mean of your favorites. this one has a second hand

what a vector is doing here

every city on a map has two numbers, its latitude and longitude. with those you can tell that london is closer to paris than to new york without knowing anything else about the three. a vector does the same job for meaning

a small language model reads an entry's headline and the first 400 characters of its blurb and hands back 384 numbers instead of two: a spot on a map of what things are about. entries about the same thing get spots near each other. a post about a chip's adder lands next to a post about the same chip's microcode, and far from a recipe

that is all the model does. it puts each entry on the map, once, on the machine that builds this page. it never sees you

an average is a taste

pick three entries. average their three vectors and you get one point, sitting between them. that point is your taste: not a profile, not a history, a spot in the same space the entries live in

cos(entry, picked) is how close an entry sits to that spot. +1 is the same direction, 0 is unrelated, and below 0 is pointed the other way. sort by it and the feed is ranked

what you pickedfeedcos to the average
loading

each pick against the average of all of them. close together and every number is high; a pick from left field pulls the average away from the rest

the catch is that one average flattens a taste with two sides. pick a recipe and a chip teardown and the average lands halfway between them, a spot that is not very close to either. so the feed also names the one pick each entry sits closest to. that is the near line: it says which of your picks this entry is like

the second hand

pass on a few entries and they average into a second point. how close an entry sits to that point, times λ, comes off its score

λ is the ruler on the feed. at 0.25, the default, a pass counts a little: an entry near what you passed loses a quarter of what it would gain by being that near what you picked. at 1 a pass counts as much as a pick. at 0 passes are ignored. the table below shows the same entry at each setting

with nothing picked there is no point to measure from, so the feed stays newest first. that is a state, not an error. and an entry with no words has no vector, so it sinks to the bottom, but nothing is ever dropped
