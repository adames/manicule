tullie murrell's hn for-you feed, cosine to the mean of your favorites

a post is a direction

start with one post. a small language model reads its headline and the first 400 characters of its blurb and writes down 384 numbers. that list is the post's vector. the model runs once, when the page is built, on the build machine. it never sees you or what you pick

you could not read the numbers and learn anything. what matters is that two posts about the same thing get similar lists, and two posts about different things get different ones. think of each list as an arrow. posts about the same thing point the same way

to compare two posts you measure the angle between their arrows. the cosine of that angle is the number you see everywhere on this site. +1 means they point the same way, 0 means a right angle, below 0 means they point apart. how long the arrows are does not matter, only the angle

the three nearest and the three farthest

in practice the numbers on this feed never use the whole range. two posts picked at random score about +0.49, and nearly every pair lands between +0.27 and +0.75. so read the numbers against that: +0.62 is close, +0.40 is far, and below 0 does not happen here

two reasons for the narrow range. with 384 numbers in each arrow, two random arrows land near a right angle almost every time, so the spread is tight. and every post here is english written by a person. that alone is something in common, and it lifts the whole feed above 0 before any subject comes into it

a post with no text gets no vector. it goes to the bottom of the feed and stays in the feed

an average is a taste

pick a post and the page keeps its vector. pick another and it adds the two lists, number by number, and divides by two. that is the average, and the average is your taste. there is no profile and no history. one list of 384 numbers that moves a little every time you pick

one average can only point one way. pick a post about sourdough and a post about compilers and the average points somewhere between them, at nothing. so the page keeps up to three averages. a pick that lands near none of them starts a new one, and every post is scored against whichever average it is closest to. if all your picks are about one thing you only ever have one average and none of this comes up

I measured this once, on a feed of about two thousand posts. with picks from two unrelated sources, one average put 13 of every 100 hidden posts in the top ten. three averages put 23 there. with picks from one source both did the same. those two numbers are from that one run. everything else on this page is computed from today's feed

cos(post, picked) is a post's angle to your average. sort the feed by it, biggest first, and that is the order you see

    the second hand

    passing on a post works the same way. the posts you pass are averaged into their own vector. cos(post, passed) is a post's angle to that one, and it comes off the score

    λ sets how much comes off. at 0.25, the default, a pass counts a quarter of what a pick does: sitting close to something you passed costs a quarter of what sitting equally close to something you picked earns. at 1 a pass cancels a pick. further down one post is worked at every setting, with a ruler you can move

    where your data lives

    your taste
    in the link and in this browser. the averages themselves, as int8 and base64, plus the ids of the rows you marked. #v=…. nothing is sent anywhere
    the vectors
    made when the page is built, by fastembed's bge-small model, on the build machine. they download as vectors.bin next to posts.json, 270 × 384 int8, and rank.js does the math in your browser
    the cli
    manicule.py rank runs on your machine. give it an opml file and a folder of notes and it writes a markdown page. the only network traffic is fetching the sources
    refresh
    once a day, by a github action. the page is rebuilt from the sources. nothing of yours goes into it
    no accounts, no server, no database
