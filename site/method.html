a post is a direction

a vector is a list of numbers. every post here has 384 of them, produced by a small language model reading the headline and the first 400 characters of the blurb. the model runs once, when this page is built. it never sees you

384 numbers are a reading on 384 scales, and together they name one direction. posts whose words mean similar things come out pointing the same way. the measure of that is the cosine of the angle between two of them: +1 is the same direction, 0 is a right angle, below 0 is opposite. every number on this site is one of those cosines

the three nearest and the three farthest

that scale is the geometry, not this feed. two posts here with nothing to do with each other score about +0.49, and 999 pairs in every 1000 fall between +0.27 and +0.75. read every number on this site inside that band: +0.62 is very near, +0.40 is far, and below 0 never happens

the band is narrow for two reasons. in 384 dimensions a random pair of directions comes out near a right angle almost every time; every angle being equally likely is true on a flat page, which is what the arrows above quietly suggest. and every post here is english written by a person, so any two share that much before they share a subject, which lifts the whole feed off 0

a post with no words has no vector, so it sinks to the bottom. it is never dropped

an average is a taste

add the vectors of everything you picked and divide by how many there are. one vector comes out. that is your taste here: no profile, no history, an average

one average can hold only one taste, because it points one way: cooking and compilers would average into a direction pointing at neither. so a pick that sits near none of your averages starts its own rather than dragging one off its subject, up to three of them, and every post is scored against the average it sits nearest. pick from one subject and nothing changes: one average, as before

measured once, on a feed of about two thousand: picking from two unrelated sources put 23 of every 100 hidden posts in the top ten, against 13 with a single average, and with picks from one source the two matched. that pair of numbers is from that one run. every other number on this page is today's

cos(post, picked) is the cosine between a post and that average. sort on it, highest first, and the feed is ordered

    the second hand

    what you pass averages the same way, into a second vector. cos(post, passed) is the cosine against that one, and λ decides how much of it comes off the score

    at 0.25, the default, a pass takes away a quarter of what the same closeness to a pick would add; at 1 the two cancel. further down there is one post worked at every setting, with the ruler that moves it

    where your data lives

    your taste
    in the link and in this browser: the averages themselves, int8 and base64, beside the ids of the rows you marked. #v=…. nothing is sent anywhere
    the vectors
    made when the page is built, by fastembed's bge-small, on the build machine. they come down in vectors.bin, next to posts.json, 270 × 384 int8, and rank.js does the math here
    the cli
    manicule.py rank, on your machine. it writes a markdown page from an opml and a folder of notes. the only traffic is the source fetches
    refresh
    once a day, by a github action. the page is rebuilt from the sources, nothing of yours goes into it
    no accounts, no server, no database
