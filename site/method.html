how it works

two averages and a subtraction

tullie murrell's hn for-you feed, cosine to the mean of your favorites

a post is an arrow

a small language model reads each post's headline and the first 400 characters of its blurb and writes down 384 numbers. that runs once, when the page is built. the model never sees you

think of the 384 numbers as an arrow. posts about the same thing point the same way. to compare two posts you measure the angle between their arrows, and the cosine of that angle is the number you see all over this site. +1 is the same direction, 0 is a right angle, below 0 is opposite

your taste is an average

pick a post and the page keeps its arrow. pick another and it averages the two. that average is your taste. no profile, no history, one arrow that moves a little every time you pick

an average can only point one way, and you are allowed to like more than one thing. so when a pick lands far from everything you have picked so far, it starts a second average instead of dragging the first off its subject. up to three. each post is scored against the one it is closest to

cos(post, picked) is a post's angle to your average. the feed is sorted by it, closest first

    the second hand

    what you pass on is averaged too, into a second arrow, and a post's closeness to that one comes off its score

    λ is how much comes off. at 0.25, the default, a pass counts a quarter of what a pick does. at 1 a pass cancels a pick. one post is worked at every setting further down, with a ruler

    where your data lives

    your taste
    in the link and in this browser. the averages themselves, as int8 and base64, plus the ids of the rows you marked. #v=…. nothing is sent anywhere
    the vectors
    made when the page is built, by fastembed's bge-small model, on the build machine. they download as vectors.bin next to posts.json, 270 × 384 int8, and rank.js does the math in your browser
    the cli
    manicule.py rank runs on your machine. give it an opml file and a folder of notes and it writes a markdown page. the only network traffic is fetching the sources
    refresh
    once a day, by a github action. the page is rebuilt from the sources. nothing of yours goes into it
    no accounts, no server, no database
