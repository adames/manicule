two averages and a subtraction

taste algorithm: tullie murrell's hn for-you feed, cosine to the mean of your favorites. this one has a second hand

a vector

a vector is a list of numbers. every post here has 384 of them, produced by a small language model reading the headline and the first 400 characters of the blurb. the model runs once, when this page is built. it never sees you

384 numbers are 384 directions, so the list is a direction. posts whose words mean similar things come out pointing the same way. the measure of that is the cosine of the angle between two of them: +1 is the same direction, 0 is unrelated, below 0 is opposite. every number on this site is one of those cosines

an average is a taste

add the vectors of everything you picked and divide by how many there are. one vector comes out. that is your taste here: no profile, no history, an average

with one exception, and it is the useful one. a pick that sits near none of your averages starts another instead of dragging one off its subject, up to three. cooking and compilers would otherwise average into a direction pointing at neither, and a post is scored against the average it is nearest. one taste behaves exactly as it always did

we tested it: readers picking from two unrelated feeds get 23 of every 100 held-out posts into the top ten, against 13 with a single average. readers picking from one feed lose nothing

cos(post, picked) is the cosine between a post and that average. sort on it, highest first, and the pool is ordered

an average points one way, so it holds one taste; that is why there can be up to three. what each one is about is read off the pool rather than stored: the feeds its nearest posts come from. the pool prints it above the rows, and it is here too

    the near line under a row names the single pick that row sits nearest, and, with several tastes, which taste. neither is a label anyone chose. it is where the vectors point

    the second hand

    what you pass on averages the same way, into a second vector. cos(post, passed) is the cosine against that one, and λ decides how much of it comes off the score

    λ is the ruler further down this page. at 0.25, the default, a pass takes away a quarter of what the same closeness to a pick would add. at 1 the two cancel. at 0 passes do nothing. the table below is one post at each setting

    with nothing picked there is no average to measure against, so the pool stays newest first. a post with no words has no vector and sinks to the bottom, but nothing is dropped

    where your data lives

    your taste
    in the link and in this browser: the averages themselves, int8 and base64, beside the ids that tick the boxes. #v=…. nothing is sent anywhere
    the vectors
    made when the page is built, by fastembed's bge-small, on the build machine. they come down in vectors.bin, next to posts.json, 270 × 384 int8, and rank.js does the math here
    the cli
    manicule.py rank, on your machine. a markdown page from an opml and a folder of notes. the only traffic is the feed fetches
    refresh
    once a day, by a github action. the page is rebuilt from the feeds, nothing of yours goes into it
    no accounts, no server, no database
